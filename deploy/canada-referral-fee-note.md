# Canada: can lawyers pay Lawvoo for referrals? (read before opening the form)

Prepared 2 October 2026. This is a plain-words summary to help you choose a
business model. It is not legal advice. Before taking money from any Canadian
law firm, have a Canadian lawyer (or the law societies themselves) confirm
the model you choose.

## The short answer

**A Canadian lawyer may not pay a non-lawyer for sending them a client.**
Lawvoo is a non-lawyer. So any model where a firm pays Lawvoo *because a
client was referred* — a commission, a share of the legal fee, a "success
fee", a fee per signed client — would put the lawyer in breach of their
professional rules. Firms will refuse it, and it is their licence at risk.

## The rule

The Federation of Law Societies' Model Code of Professional Conduct, rule
3.6-7, which the provincial law societies have adopted in their own codes:

> A lawyer must not: (a) directly or indirectly share, split, or divide his
> or her fees with any person who is not a lawyer; or (b) give any financial
> or other reward for the referral of clients or client matters to any person
> who is not a lawyer.

- Quoted as it appears in a Law Society of Newfoundland and Labrador practice
  standard, which cites its Code of Professional Conduct, s. 3.6-7:
  https://lsnl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Referral-Fee-Practice-Standard.pdf
- Model Code (PDF, see "Division of Fees and Referral Fees" in chapter 3.6):
  https://flsc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2024-Model-Code-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf
- The Federation's interactive tool shows the matching rule in every
  province's code:
  https://flsc.ca/what-we-do/model-code-of-professional-conduct/interactive-model-code-of-professional-conduct/

## What the law societies say is allowed

**Alberta** (Law Society of Alberta, "Law Business Essentials — Ethical
Obligations: Referral Fees"):
https://learningcentre.lawsociety.ab.ca/mod/page/view.php?id=674

- Lawyers cannot pay referral fees to, or split fees with, a non-lawyer who
  refers clients (rule 3.6-7).
- Lawyers **can** make "reasonable expenditures on promotional items or
  activities that may result in the referral of clients" and pay "flat fees
  for lawyer referral services".
- The key limit: the payment "cannot be directly related to a specific client
  matter, whether based on a fee per file or a percentage" of billings.

**Ontario** (Law Society of Ontario): referral fees may only be paid to
another licensed lawyer or paralegal, and even then they are capped — 15% of
the first $50,000 of fees and 5% after that, to a maximum of $25,000 — and
need a signed standard-form referral agreement. Referral fees cannot be paid
to non-licensees.

- Summary of the Ontario rules (Canadian Lawyer, 18 March 2025):
  https://www.canadianlawyermag.com/practice-areas/personal-injury/navigating-ontarios-referral-fee-landscape-a-guide-for-personal-injury-lawyers/391778
- The rule itself is in the LSO Rules of Professional Conduct, chapter 3,
  "Division of Fees and Referral Fees" (rules 3.6-5 to 3.6-7):
  https://lso.ca/about-lso/legislation-rules/rules-of-professional-conduct/chapter-3

## What this means for Lawvoo's business model

| Model | Fits the rules? |
|---|---|
| Commission, share of the legal fee, or a fee when the client signs | **No.** This is exactly what rule 3.6-7 forbids. |
| A price per enquiry passed on ("pay per lead") | **Risky.** It is not a share of fees, but it is a payment tied to a specific client. Alberta's guidance says a payment "cannot be directly related to a specific client matter, whether based on a fee per file or a percentage". Don't do this without a written opinion. |
| A flat advertising or subscription fee (same price however many enquiries or clients result) | **The model that fits.** Law societies treat reasonable spending on promotion, and flat fees for referral services, as allowed. |
| Free directory, no payment from lawyers | **No issue.** This is how the site works today. |

If you choose the flat-fee model, say so openly on the site (for example:
"Some firms pay a flat fee to advertise on Lawvoo. No firm pays per client or
per enquiry, and payment never changes the order of listings."), and update
the Terms and the /ca/listings page, which currently say firms do not pay.

## Other rules a paying firm will ask about

These come from the marketing chapters of the same codes. I did not re-read
them online for this note, so treat them as points to confirm:

- Lawyers' marketing must be true, accurate and verifiable, and not
  misleading. A firm is responsible for marketing done on its behalf — which
  includes its Lawvoo listing if it pays for it.
- A lawyer must not be described as a "specialist" or "expert" unless they
  hold a law society certification. The site only repeats a certification
  that the firm itself publishes, and never ranks lawyers as "best" or "top".
- No invented reviews or ratings. The site has none.

## What I could and could not check

- Read directly: the Newfoundland and Labrador practice standard (rule text),
  the Law Society of Alberta guidance, the Canadian Lawyer article on Ontario.
- Not read directly: the Law Society of Ontario and Law Society of British
  Columbia rule pages (they are too long for the checking tool to reach
  section 3.6). Open the links above and read rule 3.6-7 yourself, or ask a
  Canadian lawyer, before you rely on this for those two provinces.
- Not covered yet: the other provinces' own wording (Manitoba, Saskatchewan,
  Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI). They follow the Model Code, but check
  each one before taking payment from firms there.
